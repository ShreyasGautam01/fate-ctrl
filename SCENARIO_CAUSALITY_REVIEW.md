# Scenario Causality Review — The Red Umbrella

**Status:** Premise analysis; no story canon locked.  
**Purpose:** Determine whether the ordinary opening can plausibly generate the intended extreme ending, and identify which links must be established before Fate routes are designed.

## 1. The central challenge

The opening is small and emotionally legible: Jules borrowed a red umbrella, damaged it, and must return it before a flatmate leaves. The proposed ending is much larger: Jules is blamed for a citywide emergency during a live broadcast while holding the umbrella.

The danger is a chain of unrelated coincidences. The ending is only earned if each major transition:
- follows from a prior fact, choice, institution, or consequence;
- gives players a chance to notice and influence the relevant link;
- has a reason the umbrella matters beyond its color;
- allows Fate to exploit existing opportunities rather than inventing arbitrary facts;
- supports more than one causal route and at least one credible escape.

A story may be absurd at the end. Its causal structure should remain reconstructible.

## 2. Contradictions and assumptions to resolve

### Damage history
The current premise says Jules damaged the umbrella; Player B's objective says the flatmate knowingly lent an already-damaged umbrella.

Candidate resolutions:
- **Option A — Two damage events:** the umbrella had a known loose rib before borrowing; Jules later worsened a tear. This preserves both responsibility questions, but adds facts and may overload the opening.
- **Option B — Change Player B's objective:** Jules caused the damage, and Player B wants to expose the flatmate's misleading account or unfair blame. This is simpler but changes the conflict's moral center.
- **Option C — Change the premise:** the umbrella was already damaged and Jules is accused of causing the damage. This gives B a clear evidence objective, but weakens A's original responsibility for repair unless A's objective becomes about preserving trust while disputing the accusation.

**Evaluation:** Option A preserves the original conflict but creates two separate culpability questions. Option B is simpler but needs a stronger reason B opposes A. Option C has a clear evidence conflict but may make A's objective less natural. No option is accepted yet.

### Deadline
“Before the flatmate leaves” is useful pressure, but the story needs a reason the departure cannot simply be postponed. Do not solve this by making the flatmate irrational. Candidate explanations include a fixed transport departure, a work shift, or a key handover. Choose only if it affects decisions and remains consistent.

### Public broadcast and city emergency
Neither currently exists in the starting facts. They cannot be silently introduced as if already true. If the scenario needs a scheduled public event, emergency system, civic institution, or unusual city protocol, that fact must be established in the scenario before it becomes strategically relevant.

## 3. Causal bridge tests

Any proposed route from umbrella to emergency should answer each link explicitly:

1. **Ordinary object:** Why is the umbrella's condition, color, provenance, or use relevant later?
2. **Human consequence:** Which understandable choice moves the umbrella or its evidence out of the apartment context?
3. **Institutional bridge:** Which already-established organization, public process, or technical system makes that consequence matter at larger scale?
4. **Escalation trigger:** What event turns a local consequence into a wider problem?
5. **Attribution:** Why do observers connect Jules to the problem? What evidence supports that inference, and what evidence contradicts it?
6. **Publicity:** Why is a live broadcast present, and why does it cover this person or event?
7. **Fate intervention:** Which legal, limited intervention changes the probability or timing of a link without creating an unsupported fact?
8. **Player counterplay:** What can either player do at each link to redirect, delay, expose, or prevent it?
9. **Alternative route:** Can the same end-state be approached through a materially different chain of events?
10. **Escape route:** What state can produce a satisfying non-Fate ending?

If a link can be summarized only as “then a coincidence happens,” the route is not ready.

## 4. Candidate escalation architectures

These are high-level story structures to investigate, not proposed canon.

### Architecture A — Social misunderstanding becomes public narrative
The umbrella becomes part of a photograph, a public claim, or a chain of mistaken attribution. A small interpersonal dispute is amplified by an already-established public event or media process.

- **Strength:** begins with people and reputation; naturally connects to the trust/responsibility objective.
- **Risk:** can become a generic viral-misinformation plot; a citywide emergency may feel bolted on.
- **Must prove:** a concrete mechanism by which a local misunderstanding affects emergency response, rather than only online attention.

### Architecture B — Ordinary object enters a civic procedure
The umbrella crosses paths with a real, established civic process—such as a weather response, public-safety drill, transport disruption, or building evacuation—and its presence or evidence becomes consequential.

- **Strength:** creates an institutional bridge from mundane life to large consequences.
- **Risk:** the process can feel conveniently designed around one umbrella.
- **Must prove:** the procedure exists independently of Jules, has understandable rules, and remains relevant even if players take sensible actions.

### Architecture C — Human decision chain with compounding errors
Several ordinary people make individually understandable decisions based on incomplete or mistaken information. Fate's pressure exploits timing, incentives, and miscommunication rather than a magical object property.

- **Strength:** strongest fit for a strategy game about information and influence; multiple points of counterplay.
- **Risk:** harder to author and explain; can overload the player with characters and facts.
- **Must prove:** the causal chain is compact enough to remember and reconstruct.

### Architecture D — Overtly absurd but rule-consistent civic mechanism
The world contains one unusual institution or convention that is established early, with predictable rules. The ending can be ridiculous because the institution's consequences scale dramatically, not because new rules appear at the finale.

- **Strength:** supports the requested tonal escalation.
- **Risk:** worldbuilding becomes the premise instead of the relatable interpersonal conflict; a single arbitrary rule can carry the whole plot.
- **Must prove:** the unusual rule is introduced early, has limits, and creates choices rather than merely dictating the ending.

## 5. Route diversity is not route count

Three routes do not count as materially different if all three depend on the same hidden trigger and differ only in which character carries the umbrella. A useful route comparison should identify different causal mechanisms, different player counterplay, and different consequences for objectives.

For every candidate route, record:
- required facts and prerequisites;
- facts that must be known versus merely true;
- initiating action and actor;
- Fate's legal contribution and cost;
- player interventions that can break the route;
- visible warning before the point of no return;
- terminal conditions;
- how the route differs from the others.

A route graph should be derived only after the underlying causal chain is credible.

## 6. Scenario quality checks

Reject or revise a route if:
- Fate must invent a new fact after player commitment;
- a character acts on information they have not acquired;
- the umbrella matters only because the ending says it matters;
- the public blames Jules without evidence the audience can understand;
- an obvious, cheap action trivially prevents the entire scenario with no counterplay;
- all player actions merely select which forced scene occurs next;
- one objective is only achievable if another player behaves irrationally;
- the citywide emergency has no cause independent of Fate's desire for that ending;
- the non-Fate ending is just an abrupt “nothing happens” result.

## 7. Current conclusion and next work

The umbrella/transit premise is **not ready for scenario implementation**. The existing causal trace supports one developed hypothesis for public blame over a local routing failure during an independently caused storm disruption. Candidate Target 1 mechanisms now distinguish physical direction, redistribution of stale information, and post-incident attribution racing against a correction. Their current desk verdict is one plausible-but-unproven mechanism, one conditional alternate, and one weak mechanism that may need rejection. Three viable routes have not been demonstrated.

Keep three questions separate:

1. **Causal target:** Is Fate making Jules the public scapegoat for a local failure, for worsening a wider response failure, or for causing a citywide emergency? These are materially different outcomes.
2. **Route viability:** Does each proposed route have pre-existing facts, a plausible evidence chain, meaningful player intervention, a correction path, and a non-Fate ending?
3. **Objective coupling:** Do the umbrella deadline and competing player goals materially affect choices within that incident, or do they form a separate domestic subplot that merely consumes time?

The next paper exercise should compare the existing objective set against a version where Player B seeks a proportionate, evidence-backed account of the transit failure. Do not silently change `CONTROL_MODEL.md` or `scenarios/control_model_test_fixture.json`; those remain test references, not story canon. Use `CAUSAL_ROUTE_TRACE_01.md` for the detailed Target 1 trace and `CANDIDATE_CAUSAL_ROUTES.md` for alternate route mechanisms.

If fewer than three materially different routes survive skeptical reconstruction—or if the local-blame target lacks the intended scale—compare replacement premises before writing a Fate milestone graph or implementing scenario mechanics.

## 8. Objective coupling and a matched opening slice

**Status:** O1/O2 comparison hypothesis; not an approved objective change.

The current objectives may compete through time without interacting causally. That may be insufficient for a unified story. Compare these options on the same scenes and decision opportunities:

| Option | Player A | Player B | Fate | Main benefit | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **O1 — Retain current objectives** | Return the umbrella honestly and preserve trust | Establish that the flatmate knew about the old damage and had not disclosed it | Make Jules the scapegoat for the transit failure | Preserves the original intimate conflict | The umbrella evidence may not affect the public incident at all; the two stories may run in parallel |
| **O2 — Public accountability** | Return the umbrella honestly and preserve trust; may prefer a quick, private resolution | Establish an evidence-backed, proportionate account of the transit incident before the public narrative hardens | Make the public assign Jules primary responsibility while underweighting the independent causes | Shared sources, time and disclosure create direct strategic disagreement | Player B's original domestic objective is demoted or replaced |
| **O3 — Replace premise** | A new personal goal naturally connected to the crisis | A genuinely conflicting objective in that same crisis | Exploit the causal failure | Can remove the artificial bridge entirely | Requires rebuilding and retesting the opening |

### 8.1 Provisional candidate: O2

O2 is the strongest option to test on paper because the three goals can disagree for understandable reasons:

- **A values personal trust and keeping a promise.** A may try to avoid public exposure, yet support correction if Jules is unfairly blamed.
- **B values a complete and proportionate public account.** B does not need to clear Jules at any cost; if Jules contributed to the confusion, an accurate account can acknowledge it while identifying the broader causes.
- **Fate values scapegoating.** It wants the public to assign Jules primary responsibility while losing sight of the stale display, closure timing and other established contributors. It cannot change the actual cause of the incident.

B and Fate may both seek public attention, but their reasons and desired account differ. A and B may cooperate on correction, then disagree if the evidence exposes Jules's own risky action. This is a more useful conflict than simply assigning each player a different meter.

### 8.3 Acceptance gate

O1 and O2 should be judged against the same questions:

1. Can each player explain their goal in one plain sentence?
2. Do the first three decisions affect both personal stakes and the public incident, rather than splitting into unrelated subplots?
3. Can the players disagree without either acting irrationally?
4. Can an accurate account implicate Jules partly while still defeating disproportionate scapegoating?
5. Does the umbrella deadline create a real opportunity cost without forcing Jules into the incident?
6. Can both a Fate-success and a non-Fate ending resolve the personal promise and public incident?
7. Can an uncoached reader reconstruct the causal chain without author explanation, “then it goes viral,” or an unseeded source?

**Pass:** at least two meaningful disagreements, clear evidence ownership, credible counterplay and a full non-Fate ending can be reconstructed.

**Fail:** the umbrella is merely a decorative identifier, private umbrella evidence has no consequential relationship to the public incident, or Jules must remain at the interchange for the plot to work. Test O3 and replace the premise rather than coding around these failures.

O2 is a candidate for the next paper iteration only. No objective change is approved, and no production mechanics should be implemented until the route and objective-coupling gates pass.

### 9.1 Current desk verdict by gate

- **Causal distinction:** partially improved. T1-A and T1-B use different causal hinges; T1-C remains weak and may collapse into an attribution variant unless its source is concretely established.
- **Objective coupling:** O2 is stronger than O1 as a shared action economy, but the umbrella currently supplies pressure more than causal connection. This is not yet a decisive win for O2.
- **Direct-action preference:** retained as the leading interaction hypothesis. It is not yet a selected production control model.
- **Story/UI requirements:** story-first narration, hidden intentions, and a small number of readable condition indicators remain requirements from owner feedback. They are not satisfied by this paper document.
- **Implementation readiness:** **not ready**. The next useful artifact should be a compact, fully worked three-beat paper round that defines both player intentions, the resolved protagonist action, knowledge updates, the observable story paragraph, and only the state indicators changed by that event.

### 9.2 Next paper-round acceptance test

Use one worked round under O2, without coding:

1. Show the same public scene to both players, then provide distinct private objectives.
2. Have each player submit one direct intention without seeing the other's submission.
3. Resolve to one protagonist action using a rule that can be reconstructed from the fixture, not a narrative judgment.
4. Write the story paragraph from the resolved event and facts actually known to Jules.
5. Update at most three visible state indicators, each with a one-line causal explanation.
6. Reveal only the observable action and consequence—not either player's submitted intention or the hidden selection diagnostics.
7. Ask a reviewer what happened, what Jules knew, what changed, why the indicators moved, and what each player might try next.

**Pass:** the reviewer can explain the event and indicators without being shown the hidden submissions; both player intentions remain inferable only from observed behavior; the scene creates a plausible next disagreement.

**Fail:** the story reveals player inputs, the meter change is not grounded in the event, the protagonist acts on unknown information, or the resolution can only be justified by saying the selector chose it. In that case, fix the action contract before building a richer browser UI.
