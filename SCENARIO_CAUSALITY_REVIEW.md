# Scenario Causality Review — The Red Umbrella

**Status:** Premise analysis; no story canon locked.  
**Purpose:** Determine whether the ordinary opening can plausibly generate the intended extreme ending, and identify which links must be established before Fate routes are designed.

## 1. The central challenge

The opening is small and emotionally legible: Jules borrowed a red umbrella, damaged it, and must return it before a flatmate leaves. The proposed ending is much larger: Jules is blamed for a citywide emergency during a live broadcast while holding the umbrella.

The danger is a chain of unrelated coincidences. The ending is only earned if each major transition:
- follows from a prior fact, choice, institution, or consequence;
- gives players a chance to notice and influence the relevant link;
- has a reason the umbrella matters beyond its color;
- allows Fate to exploit existing opportunities rather than inventing arbitrary facts;
- supports more than one causal route and at least one credible escape.

A story may be absurd at the end. Its causal structure should remain reconstructible.

## 2. Contradictions and assumptions to resolve

### Damage history
The current premise says Jules damaged the umbrella; Player B's objective says the flatmate knowingly lent an already-damaged umbrella.

Candidate resolutions:
- **Option A — Two damage events:** the umbrella had a known loose rib before borrowing; Jules later worsened a tear. This preserves both responsibility questions, but adds facts and may overload the opening.
- **Option B — Change Player B's objective:** Jules caused the damage, and Player B wants to expose the flatmate's misleading account or unfair blame. This is simpler but changes the conflict's moral center.
- **Option C — Change the premise:** the umbrella was already damaged and Jules is accused of causing the damage. This gives B a clear evidence objective, but weakens A's original responsibility for repair unless A's objective becomes about preserving trust while disputing the accusation.

**Evaluation:** Option A preserves the original conflict but creates two separate culpability questions. Option B is simpler but needs a stronger reason B opposes A. Option C has a clear evidence conflict but may make A's objective less natural. No option is accepted yet.

### Deadline
“Before the flatmate leaves” is useful pressure, but the story needs a reason the departure cannot simply be postponed. Do not solve this by making the flatmate irrational. Candidate explanations include a fixed transport departure, a work shift, or a key handover. Choose only if it affects decisions and remains consistent.

### Public broadcast and city emergency
Neither currently exists in the starting facts. They cannot be silently introduced as if already true. If the scenario needs a scheduled public event, emergency system, civic institution, or unusual city protocol, that fact must be established in the scenario before it becomes strategically relevant.

## 3. Causal bridge tests

Any proposed route from umbrella to emergency should answer each link explicitly:

1. **Ordinary object:** Why is the umbrella's condition, color, provenance, or use relevant later?
2. **Human consequence:** Which understandable choice moves the umbrella or its evidence out of the apartment context?
3. **Institutional bridge:** Which already-established organization, public process, or technical system makes that consequence matter at larger scale?
4. **Escalation trigger:** What event turns a local consequence into a wider problem?
5. **Attribution:** Why do observers connect Jules to the problem? What evidence supports that inference, and what evidence contradicts it?
6. **Publicity:** Why is a live broadcast present, and why does it cover this person or event?
7. **Fate intervention:** Which legal, limited intervention changes the probability or timing of a link without creating an unsupported fact?
8. **Player counterplay:** What can either player do at each link to redirect, delay, expose, or prevent it?
9. **Alternative route:** Can the same end-state be approached through a materially different chain of events?
10. **Escape route:** What state can produce a satisfying non-Fate ending?

If a link can be summarized only as “then a coincidence happens,” the route is not ready.

## 4. Candidate escalation architectures

These are high-level story structures to investigate, not proposed canon.

### Architecture A — Social misunderstanding becomes public narrative
The umbrella becomes part of a photograph, a public claim, or a chain of mistaken attribution. A small interpersonal dispute is amplified by an already-established public event or media process.

- **Strength:** begins with people and reputation; naturally connects to the trust/responsibility objective.
- **Risk:** can become a generic viral-misinformation plot; a citywide emergency may feel bolted on.
- **Must prove:** a concrete mechanism by which a local misunderstanding affects emergency response, rather than only online attention.

### Architecture B — Ordinary object enters a civic procedure
The umbrella crosses paths with a real, established civic process—such as a weather response, public-safety drill, transport disruption, or building evacuation—and its presence or evidence becomes consequential.

- **Strength:** creates an institutional bridge from mundane life to large consequences.
- **Risk:** the process can feel conveniently designed around one umbrella.
- **Must prove:** the procedure exists independently of Jules, has understandable rules, and remains relevant even if players take sensible actions.

### Architecture C — Human decision chain with compounding errors
Several ordinary people make individually understandable decisions based on incomplete or mistaken information. Fate's pressure exploits timing, incentives, and miscommunication rather than a magical object property.

- **Strength:** strongest fit for a strategy game about information and influence; multiple points of counterplay.
- **Risk:** harder to author and explain; can overload the player with characters and facts.
- **Must prove:** the causal chain is compact enough to remember and reconstruct.

### Architecture D — Overtly absurd but rule-consistent civic mechanism
The world contains one unusual institution or convention that is established early, with predictable rules. The ending can be ridiculous because the institution's consequences scale dramatically, not because new rules appear at the finale.

- **Strength:** supports the requested tonal escalation.
- **Risk:** worldbuilding becomes the premise instead of the relatable interpersonal conflict; a single arbitrary rule can carry the whole plot.
- **Must prove:** the unusual rule is introduced early, has limits, and creates choices rather than merely dictating the ending.

## 5. Route diversity is not route count

Three routes do not count as materially different if all three depend on the same hidden trigger and differ only in which character carries the umbrella. A useful route comparison should identify different causal mechanisms, different player counterplay, and different consequences for objectives.

For every candidate route, record:
- required facts and prerequisites;
- facts that must be known versus merely true;
- initiating action and actor;
- Fate's legal contribution and cost;
- player interventions that can break the route;
- visible warning before the point of no return;
- terminal conditions;
- how the route differs from the others.

A route graph should be derived only after the underlying causal chain is credible.

## 6. Scenario quality checks

Reject or revise a route if:
- Fate must invent a new fact after player commitment;
- a character acts on information they have not acquired;
- the umbrella matters only because the ending says it matters;
- the public blames Jules without evidence the audience can understand;
- an obvious, cheap action trivially prevents the entire scenario with no counterplay;
- all player actions merely select which forced scene occurs next;
- one objective is only achievable if another player behaves irrationally;
- the citywide emergency has no cause independent of Fate's desire for that ending;
- the non-Fate ending is just an abrupt “nothing happens” result.

## 7. Current conclusion and next work

Do not finalize the umbrella facts or write a milestone graph yet. First choose a coherent damage-history option, define the relationship and deadline in human terms, and generate at least three short causal-chain candidates. Compare them on plausibility, memorability, strategic branching, visibility, counterplay, objective interaction, and authoring cost.

If no causal bridge survives those checks, replace the scenario or revise Fate's terminal goal. Protect the game's core fantasy rather than forcing a weak premise to survive.
